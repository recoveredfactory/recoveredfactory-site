# Daybook video music

The working palette for scoring the dossier/carousel videos: **atmospheric,
subdued, off-kilter, low drama, but engaging.** Dry menace over wet drama — the
documents are the drama, the music is the deadpan. Never the obvious film-score
reach (no swelling-strings investigation music, no "Warren Ellis").

House style is the **International Anthem lane** (Makaya McCraven, Jeff Parker,
Alabaster DePlume) — decided 2026-08-13, anchored on DePlume's **"Visit
Croatia"** (*To Cy & Lee: Instrumentals Vol. 1*). The house style is the
product's taste; personal one-off picks (Neubauten, DOOM, Ela Minus) stay in
rotation for editions that call for them. IA and Thrill Jockey both being small
Chicago-orbit indies keeps licensing a realistic conversation.

## "Visit Croatia" neighbors (shortlist, 2026-08-14)

The tender pole of the palette. For the dry-menace pole, reach for jaimie
branch's quieter *Fly or Die* cuts instead.

- **Sam Gendel & Sam Wilkes** — *Music for Saxofone & Bass Guitar* ("Boa") —
  nearest living relatives: woozy sax over patient bass, casually recorded
- **Alabaster DePlume** — "Whisky Story Time", "Song of the Foundling" (*To Cy
  & Lee*); "Don't Forget You're Precious" if a little vocal is fine
- **Sam Wilkes** — "One Theme & Subsequent Improvisation" — same family, more
  spacious
- **Jeff Parker** — "Build a Nest", "After the Rain" (*Suite for Max Brown*) —
  subdued, loop-brained, warm
- **Resavoir** — "Escalator" — Chicago, hazy, short
- **Carlos Niño & Friends** — "Actually" — looser, same unhurried lilt
- **Nala Sinephro** — *Space 1.8* ("Space 1", "Space 3") — harp/sax ambient
  jazz; the most scoreable thing on this list
- **Green-House** — "Peperomia Seedling" — naive melody on synth; sits under
  narration
- **Shabaka** — "End of Innocence" (*Perceive Its Beauty, Acknowledge Its
  Grace*) — flutes, hushed
- **Mary Lattimore** — "It Feels Like Floating" — harp, wistful, long arcs

**Under-narration picks** (duck under spoken word without dying): Sinephro,
Green-House, Lattimore. The Gendel/Wilkes and DePlume material has enough
personality to fight narration — better under document pans and title cards.
