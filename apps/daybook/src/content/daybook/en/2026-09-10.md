## USCIS says Salvadorans with TPS remain protected. But its online assurance is not a legal notice.

**USCIS says Salvadorans with TPS remain protected, including their authorization to work. But the online assurance is not itself the formal action that extends or ends TPS.** The agency says another announcement will come “at the appropriate time.” That is the federal government’s current instruction, not a published extension or termination notice. [USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/humanitarian/temporary-protected-status/temporary-protected-status-designated-country-el-salvador), [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/09/us/el-salvador-tps-deadline), [Truthout](https://truthout.org/articles/dhs-claims-tps-for-170000-salvadorans-will-remain-despite-looming-expiration/). **What is TPS?** [American Immigration Council](https://www.americanimmigrationcouncil.org/fact-sheet/temporary-protected-status-tps-overview/)

**Honduras and Nicaragua offer a limited recent comparison.** DHS published their termination notices on July 8, 2025—three days after the listed July 5 end date—and made the terminations effective September 8. That is not a forecast for El Salvador, and the notices do not say when the secretary made the underlying determination. But they show DHS recently announcing TPS terminations after a listed deadline, with a formal notice setting a later operative date. [Honduras notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/07/08/2025-12621/termination-of-the-designation-of-honduras-for-temporary-protected-status), [Nicaragua notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/07/08/2025-12688/termination-of-the-designation-of-nicaragua-for-temporary-protected-status)

**In a first for the newsletter, a significant cluster of publications got it wrong** and said that the status was definitively expiring. But the deadline—a public-facing date with several possible legal outcomes—arrived without a formal notice or clear agency explanation. That made for a fiendishly confusing situation for reporters and readers alike. How will those publications tell their audiences what changed, and how will readers distinguish that correction from the next rumor? The unresolved question remains what DHS means by “the appropriate time”—and what formal action and document instructions will follow.

## Phoenix’s “temporary” ICE holding site is drawing new scrutiny.

**The Phoenix ICE District Office has 10 holding cells and is supposed to hold people no more than 12 hours.** It has no beds or on-site medical services. KJZZ’s review of Phoenix Fire Department records found 11 emergency responses there from December through March, nearly all ending in Code 3 ambulance transport to a hospital. [KJZZ](https://www.kjzz.org/the-show/2026-09-09/an-ice-holding-facility-in-phoenix-is-racking-up-911-calls-for-medical-emergencies)

**Rep. Yassamin Ansari visited Wednesday, after Arizona Mirror’s reporting, and said she found 29 people there.** The Mirror reported average stays had climbed from roughly eight to 10 hours to about 20; Ansari said ICE officials told her a waiver of the 12-hour limit had been lifted in July. ICE disputes the Mirror’s reading of its data and says the office does not hold people longer than 12 hours. [Arizona Mirror](https://azmirror.com/2026/09/09/ansari-makes-surprise-visit-to-phoenix-ice-facility-after-mirror-report), [KTAR News](https://ktar.com/immigration/ice-oversight-visit-yassamin-ansari-phoenix)

## A judge asks whether children are actually getting lawyers.

**A federal judge is demanding more evidence that the government is maintaining legal aid for unaccompanied children facing immigration court alone.** The Office of Refugee Resettlement ended its contract with a nationwide network of more than 80 legal-service groups in July and turned to USCRI, whose official said it was still ramping up. USCRI lawyers have appeared in 1,600 hearings as Friends of Court since early August, but that role can explain a proceeding or identify a child’s language; it cannot file motions or other documents for the child. The judge ordered fuller evidence later this month. [KJZZ](https://kjzz.org/fronteras-desk/2026-09-09/advocates-say-legal-aid-program-for-migrant-kids-is-still-in-disarray)

## Around the system


**Hartford, Connecticut:** The ACLU of Connecticut sued the immigration court over access to dockets, case records and in-absentia removal orders, arguing that the public has a First Amendment right to records used to decide individual cases. [New Haven Register](https://nhregister.com/news/article/hartford-immigration-court-aclu-lawsuit-22424005.php)

**Oklahoma:** An analysis of Deportation Data Project records found more than 1,800 immigration-related arrests from January through July tied to local partnerships with federal immigration authorities; fewer than 20% of those arrested had a criminal conviction, according to Oklahoma Voice. [Oklahoma Voice](https://newsfromthestates.com/article/local-partnerships-ice-result-record-number-immigration-arrests-oklahoma)

**Danville, Pennsylvania:** Advocates say ICE arrested at least 49 construction workers in two operations connected to the Geisinger Medical Center expansion. The hospital said the initial law-enforcement activity did not occur on its property; ICE and DHS did not immediately respond to requests for comment. [The Philadelphia Inquirer](https://inquirer.com/news/ice-arrests-immigrants-danville-pennsylvania-20260908.html), [WVIA](https://wvia.org/news/local/2026-09-09/immigrant-advocates-say-ice-arrested-dozens-at-geisinger-medical-center-construction-site-in-danville)

**South Portland, Maine:** City councilors voted to move a proposed ordinance limiting city-employee cooperation with federal immigration enforcement to an October workshop. It is a live local-policy discussion, not an adopted ordinance. [WMTW](https://wmtw.com/article/south-portland-maine-ordinance-city-employees-immigration-enforcement/73646423)

**Minnesota:** The state and the Trump administration argued over whether Minnesota’s challenge to the concluded Operation Metro Surge can continue. The federal government sought dismissal; the state said there was no commitment that the challenged tactics would not resume. [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/minnesota-trump-administration-clash-over-future-of-immigration-surge-suit/)

**Gilroy, California:** A federal judge is expected to rule September 15 on the proposed ICE detention facility near Gilroy after Santa Clara County raised capacity and environmental concerns. [KCRA](https://www.kcra.com/article/gilroy-proposed-ice-detention-center-pushback/73655471)
