## Second death at Newark's Delaney Hall detention center intensifies scrutiny of ICE custody

**A second person held at the Delaney Hall immigration facility in Newark, New Jersey, has died** after being transferred to a hospital, according to Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) and his family. The death adds a fresh human toll to a beat that has moved from arrest counts to what happens once people are locked up, amid parallel litigation and congressional oversight of custody conditions nationwide. [Gothamist](https://gothamist.com/news/second-detainee-dies-after-being-held-at-delaney-hall-ice-detention-center), [Austin Kocher](https://austinkocher.substack.com/p/edwin-lopez-cornejo-is-the-23rd-person)

**The American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU) and civil rights groups sued** the Department of Homeland Security over conditions they call inhumane at the nation's largest immigration detention facility, in Texas. The suit joins a broader wave of litigation testing whether federal courts will step in over overcrowding and medical neglect. [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/aclu-sues-dhs-inhumane-conditions-nations-largest-immigration/story?id=133492027)

A Senate staff report on conditions at the Dilley, Texas, family detention facility **described a "top-down culture of cruelty,"** and Sen. Dick Durbin is pressing for a full Senate hearing. Advocates say the documentation strengthens calls for independent inspection across a detention system that spans more than two hundred sites. [Talking Points Memo](https://talkingpointsmemo.com/news/senate-report-describes-top-down-culture-of-cruelty-in-ice-detention)

**Prisciliano Trejo, a 29-year-old Mexican national with leukemia, died after ICE released him** to his family while he was already in a coma, according to community groups who say he went eight days without treatment at Georgia's Stewart Detention Center. His case is fueling diplomatic tension with Mexico and may never appear on ICE's official death tally, because he was technically no longer in custody when he died, a gap that Spanish-language outlets have been tracking. [El País](https://english.elpais.com/usa/2026-07-31/the-agonizing-death-of-prisciliano-trejo-ice-released-him-to-his-family-while-he-was-in-a-coma.html), [Univision](https://univision.com/local/atlanta-wuvg/prisciliano-trejo-murio-de-leucemia-bajo-custodia-de-ice-familia-pide-investigar-atencion-y-autoridades-aseguran-que-brindaron-atencion-oportuna)

**From the Recovered Factory knowledgebase: **ICE's own Office of Detention Oversight last inspected Delaney Hall in late August 2025 and rated it Acceptable/Adequate while still logging 12 deficiencies, including three priority findings on hold-room checks, cold-food temperatures, and suicide-watch documentation. The report is the baseline public compliance record for the Newark facility as deaths and political scrutiny mount. [ICE ODO — Delaney Hall, Aug. 26–28, 2025](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/foia/odo-compliance-inspections/DelaneyHallDetFac_NewarkNJ_08262025.pdf)

## Immigration courts accelerate as ICE posts record arrest month

**ICE booked more than 46,000 people into detention in July** according to internal figures obtained by CBS News, averaging about 1,500 book-ins a day as enforcement widened to airports, check-in offices, and neighborhoods. Agents deployed to at least 15 airports have made arrests using Transportation Security Administration (TSA) travel data, part of a push toward the administration's stated goal of one million deportations a year. [CBS News](https://cbsnews.com/news/ice-detentions-trump-high-july-2026-immigration-crackdown-widens)

**Immigration courts are packing dockets and cutting notice windows,** with more than 1,300 "mega" master hearings held in June. The compressed pace is driving up both missed court dates and immediate deportation orders as the system strains to clear a decades-long backlog. [AP](https://apnews.com/article/trump-immigration-deportation-asylum-judges-82d6b7e79f4fd12856ff1c5ad66f2445), [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/wireStory/breakneck-pace-immigration-courts-driving-deportation-orders-135280987)

Federal appeals courts in Chicago and San Francisco both **rejected the administration's mandatory-detention policy on the same day**, deepening a split that now has six circuits against the policy and two in favor. The rulings may open bond hearings for people detained in Illinois, Indiana, Wisconsin, and other states in those circuits, though the Supreme Court is expected to settle the question. [Los Angeles Times](https://latimes.com/california/story/2026-07-31/trump-mandatory-immigrant-detention-policy-9th-circuit-ruling)

## Miami activists press City Hall to scrap 287(g) pact as Haitian status crisis deepens in South Florida

Community leaders and Florida State Rep. Angie Nixon rallied outside Miami City Hall to **demand the city end its voluntary 287(g) agreement**. The program deputizes local police to help ICE with federal immigration enforcement. Organizers argue the pact erodes trust between immigrant residents and the officers meant to protect them. [Miami Herald](https://miamiherald.com/news/local/immigration/article316727439.html), [Telemundo 51 (Miami)](https://youtube.com/watch?v=khs1leHG79s)

Among U.S. states, **Florida has the highest share of its law-enforcement agencies enrolled in 287(g)**, according to our own [287(g) Watch](https://287g.recoveredfactory.net/en/state/fl).
