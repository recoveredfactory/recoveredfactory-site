## A Colorado judge orders DHS to bring back a Honduran man sent to Africa

**A federal judge ordered DHS to return Brayan Omar Sanchez Servellon to Colorado after he was sent to the Central African Republic, a country he had never visited.** Sanchez Servellon, a Honduran man with work authorization who had lived in the United States for about a decade, had protection under the Convention Against Torture from being returned to Honduras. Judge Regina Rodriguez found that he received neither notice nor a chance to challenge his transfer to a third country. [Colorado Public Radio](https://www.cpr.org/2026/09/24/judge-order-colorado-man-deported-africa-return/)

**The remedy is unusually concrete: return him to Colorado and release him with his belongings, without GPS monitoring, mandatory reporting or other movement restrictions.** The order makes the immediate dispute less abstract—what process the government owes before it sends someone to a country with which they have no connection—while the administration separately seeks Supreme Court permission to continue rapid third-country removals more broadly. [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/judge-orders-return-honduran-migrant-deported-central-african/story?id=136626492), [EL PAÍS](https://english.elpais.com/usa/2026-09-24/for-the-first-time-court-orders-us-government-to-return-migrant-deported-to-africa.html)

## Cattle groups say ICE operations are disrupting the beef supply chain

**Three cattle associations say recent ICE activity in Oklahoma, Kansas and Texas has delayed shipments of thousands of fed cattle, costing producers millions and disrupting feedyards, dairies, processors, feed and grain businesses, transportation hubs and community services.** The Kansas Livestock Association, Oklahoma Cattlemen’s Association and Texas Cattle Feeders Association said the operations were having a “massive chilling effect” on legal, documented, skilled workers who keep the supply chain moving. [KOCO](https://www.koco.com/article/oklahoma-cattlemens-association-ice-raids-beef-industry/73876478), [Oklahoma Farm Report](https://www.oklahomafarmreport.com/2026/09/24/cattle-groups-voice-concern-about-market-disruptions-in-the-southern-plains/), [MyHighPlains](https://www.myhighplains.com/news/local-news/texas-oklahoma-kansas-cattle-associations-release-joint-statement-on-area-ice-operations/)

**The feedyard disruptions have also drawn their own industry coverage.** [Drovers](https://www.drovers.com/news/ice-raids-ag-facilities-southwest-kansas) reported on raids at agricultural facilities in southwest Kansas, while [HPPR](https://www.hppr.org/2026-09-23/ice-agents-in-kansas-cause-schools-to-warn-families-and-meatpacking-plants-to-delay-shifts) reported that ICE activity prompted schools to warn families and meatpacking plants to delay shifts.

The region around Dodge City, Garden City and Liberal accounts for about 22% of the country’s grain-fed beef supply; local officials said they had not been told in advance about the operation, and National Beef delayed a shift at its Liberal, Kansas, plant. [KCUR](https://www.kcur.org/politics-elections-and-government/2026-09-24/kansas-residents-are-reeling-amid-sweeping-ice-enforcement-action-at-meat-processing-plants), [Meatingplace](https://meatingplace.com/ice-activity-in-kansas-affects-national-beef-production-report/)

## ICE awards a $125 million surveillance contract to Thomson Reuters

**ICE is days away from beginning work on a $125 million Thomson Reuters Special Services contract that the procurement describes as a way to continuously monitor up to 1 million people and flag potentially criminal or fraudulent behavior.** That is the stated capability of the planned service, not proof it is already monitoring people at that scale; FedScoop reports it follows a previous CACI continuous-monitoring contract and that Thomson Reuters was the sole bidder after the requirements changed. [FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/dhs-ice-surveillance-caci-thomson-reuters/)

## A permanent injunction requires changes at 26 Federal Plaza

**A federal judge permanently barred ICE from overcrowding Manhattan holding rooms at 26 Federal Plaza and required safeguards including sanitation, meals, medical care and a confidential, free call to a lawyer within an hour of arrival.** Judge Lewis A. Kaplan’s order follows findings that people had been held well beyond short processing stays and that ICE had not reliably complied with earlier relief; the 125-page ruling establishes continuing court oversight. [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/in-final-ruling-against-ice-judge-blasts-dreadful-and-squalid-conditions-at-26-federal-plaza/), [Documented](https://documentedny.com/2026/09/24/judge-orders-ice-detention-improvements)
