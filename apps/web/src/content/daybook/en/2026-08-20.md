## Lawsuit challenges fixed admission periods for students, exchange visitors and journalists

**A coalition sued the Department of Homeland Security on Tuesday, following [Monday’s announcement](https://recoveredfactory.net/en/daybook/2026-08-18), to block the fixed-stay policy for students, exchange visitors, researchers and foreign journalists.** The case challenges the rule replacing duration-of-status admission with fixed periods. [El País](https://english.elpais.com/usa/2026-08-19/coalition-sues-trump-administration-over-limits-on-foreign-student-visa-stays.html), [Forbes](https://forbes.com/sites/stuartanderson/2026/08/18/university-groups-file-immigration-lawsuit-to-block-student-rule), [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/19/us-unions-sue-over-new-visa-rule-for-foreign-students-journalists)

**For people in longer F and J visa programs, staying beyond the fixed period could mean filing an extension request with USCIS.** Students who need more time would have to apply for an extension of stay, potentially adding fees, biometrics and an interview to programs that run longer than the initial admission period. [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-labor-report/unions-college-groups-challenge-limits-on-foreign-students)

## Mexico presses for accountability in deaths tied to U.S. immigration custody and operations

**English-language coverage of Mexico’s response has been thin, but El País is reporting an accumulating legal and diplomatic effort over 17 Mexican deaths connected to ICE custody or operations.** Mexico has filed 20 complaints with U.S. authorities, requested a Justice Department investigation and raised the cases with the United Nations. [El País](https://english.elpais.com/international/2026-08-19/mexico-steps-up-legal-offensive-over-17-mexicans-who-died-in-ice-custody-there-are-too-many-cases.html), [El Mañana de Reynosa](https://elmanana.com/noticias/nacional/muerte-de-mexicanos-bajo-custodia-ice-sre-investiga/6175323)

**The campaign is still developing, not a settled international human-rights finding.** Its next significance depends on how U.S. prosecutors, courts and international bodies respond to Mexico’s complaints and requests for investigation. [Enfoque Noticias](https://enfoquenoticias.com.mx/noticias/mexico-denunciara-muertes-de-17-mexicanos-en-centros-de-detencion-de-eu), [La Chispa](https://lachispa.mx/mexico-escala-defensa-por-muertes-de-connacionales-bajo-custodia)

## Court-ordered oversight puts immigrant children's detention conditions under new scrutiny

**A judge has ordered independent monitoring and closer court supervision after findings about conditions in facilities holding immigrant children.** The order makes the government’s compliance with detention standards an immediate issue for children in those facilities. [The New York Times](https://nytimes.com/2026/08/18/us/judge-children-immigration-detention-order.html)

**The oversight order arrives amid separate concerns about legal representation and plans for additional child-detention capacity.** Those are distinct developments, but together they put both the conditions of custody and the safeguards available to unaccompanied children under pressure. [American Immigration Council](https://americanimmigrationcouncil.org/blog/trump-administration-makes-it-harder-for-unaccompanied-children-to-navigate-the-immigration-courts-by-attacking-their-right-to-counsel), [El Espectador](https://elespectador.com/mundo/america/trump-planea-crear-centros-de-detencion-para-menores-migrantes-no-acompanados)
