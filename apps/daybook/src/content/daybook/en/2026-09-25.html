A Colorado judge orders DHS to bring back a Honduran man sent to Africa

A federal judge ordered DHS to return Brayan Omar Sanchez Servellon to Colorado after he was sent to the Central African Republic, a country he had never visited. Sanchez Servellon, a Honduran man with work authorization who had lived in the United States for about a decade, had protection under the Convention Against Torture from being returned to Honduras. Judge Regina Rodriguez found that he received neither notice nor a chance to challenge his transfer to a third country. Colorado Public Radio

The remedy is unusually concrete: return him to Colorado and release him with his belongings, without GPS monitoring, mandatory reporting or other movement restrictions. The order makes the immediate dispute less abstract—what process the government owes before it sends someone to a country with which they have no connection—while the administration separately seeks Supreme Court permission to continue rapid third-country removals more broadly. ABC News, EL PAÍS

Cattle groups say ICE operations are disrupting the beef supply chain

Three cattle associations say recent ICE activity in Oklahoma, Kansas and Texas has delayed shipments of thousands of fed cattle, costing producers millions and disrupting feedyards, dairies, processors, feed and grain businesses, transportation hubs and community services. The Kansas Livestock Association, Oklahoma Cattlemen’s Association and Texas Cattle Feeders Association said the operations were having a “massive chilling effect” on legal, documented, skilled workers who keep the supply chain moving. KOCO, Oklahoma Farm Report, MyHighPlains

The feedyard disruptions have also drawn their own industry coverage. Drovers reported on raids at agricultural facilities in southwest Kansas, while HPPR reported that ICE activity prompted schools to warn families and meatpacking plants to delay shifts.

The region around Dodge City, Garden City and Liberal accounts for about 22% of the country’s grain-fed beef supply; local officials said they had not been told in advance about the operation, and National Beef delayed a shift at its Liberal, Kansas, plant. KCUR, Meatingplace

ICE awards a $125 million surveillance contract to Thomson Reuters

ICE is days away from beginning work on a $125 million Thomson Reuters Special Services contract that the procurement describes as a way to continuously monitor up to 1 million people and flag potentially criminal or fraudulent behavior. That is the stated capability of the planned service, not proof it is already monitoring people at that scale; FedScoop reports it follows a previous CACI continuous-monitoring contract and that Thomson Reuters was the sole bidder after the requirements changed. FedScoop

A permanent injunction requires changes at 26 Federal Plaza

A federal judge permanently barred ICE from overcrowding Manhattan holding rooms at 26 Federal Plaza and required safeguards including sanitation, meals, medical care and a confidential, free call to a lawyer within an hour of arrival. Judge Lewis A. Kaplan’s order follows findings that people had been held well beyond short processing stays and that ICE had not reliably complied with earlier relief; the 125-page ruling establishes continuing court oversight. Courthouse News, Documented

Applying to asylum: People who apply for asylum on their own with USCIS can now have their cases referred straight to immigration court without an interview first. The interim rule, already in effect, also removes the requirement that a referral letter assess the applicant’s credibility. Comments close September 28.

Fines for contempt: Immigration judges would be able to fine people who defy their authority in court (contempt), under a proposed rule setting out who can be penalised, how much, and how to appeal — aimed mainly at lawyers and others appearing in deportation cases. Comments can be filed until 11:59 p.m. Eastern on September 28, 2026.
Federal Register

Employment-based visas: A new fiscal year's supply of employment-based green card numbers becomes available on October 1, so the October 2026 Visa Bulletin — the State Department chart that sets who can file or be approved based on their place in line (priority date) — may show forward movement in backlogged categories such as EB-2 India.
WR Immigration (Wolfsdorf Rosenthal)

Shelter inspections: The federal office that houses immigrant children who arrive without a parent or guardian, the Office of Refugee Resettlement (ORR), wants to revise the forms its inspectors use to check whether shelters are meeting licensing rules and other legal requirements. Anyone can comment until October 5, 2026.
Federal Register

Online filing: USCIS can now require that certain applications and petitions be filed online rather than on paper, and the rule sets out how it will designate which ones. People who cannot file electronically can ask for a waiver. The change is already in force as an interim final rule, but comments are open until October 13, 2026.

Around the system

  • More on the shooting in Austin, TX: A specialist said Wilber Rafael Garces Perez needs surgery to remove a bullet lodged near his spine, Rep. Greg Casar said after visiting him in ICE custody. Garces Perez was shot by an ICE officer during a September 20 operation. A federal judge has temporarily halted his removal while a September 30 habeas hearing is pending. NBC News / AP, Austin American-Statesman

  • The Justice Department announced $25 million in ANGEL grants for state, local and tribal agencies that assist immigration enforcement. The program adds a new federal funding route for participating law-enforcement agencies. Valley News Live

  • The Fifth Circuit heard an en banc challenge over immigration detention without bond hearings. The case asks whether longtime U.S. residents can challenge the government’s claim that continued detention is necessary. American Immigration Council

  • A state-commissioned Maine report estimated that immigration enforcement could remove up to 7,000 workers and $3.3 billion in annual economic output. The analysis pointed to labor shortages in sectors already struggling to hire. Central Maine

  • San Diego’s Haitian community is confronting the practical aftermath of the administration’s termination of Haiti’s Temporary Protected Status designation. KPBS reported on residents navigating the loss of protection and its effects on work, families and community life. KPBS

  • Aminata Seck, the Senegalese college basketball player, remains in ICE custody pending removal proceedings, according to DHS. Her detention has drawn attention beyond immigration coverage because of her place in women’s college basketball. USA Today

  • A lawsuit challenges DHS’s expanded use of GPS monitoring, arguing that electronic monitoring decisions must be individualized rather than imposed broadly. LA NACION
